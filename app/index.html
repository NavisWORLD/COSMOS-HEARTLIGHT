Color modulation rule

The app never assumes one color is “calming” for everyone. It combines the room reading with the child’s selected needs and accessibility settings. If brightness is high and “too bright” is selected, it can suggest reducing glare or offering a lower-light option. The child’s preference wins.

Study arc

  1. Observe without labeling.
  2. Ask what the learner is communicating.
  3. Separate sensory load, task demand, communication load, and regulation need.
  4. Offer choices and access supports.
  5. Record only what is necessary.
  6. Review patterns with the student and team.
  7. Test one small change at a time.
  8. Keep what helps; discard what does not.

COSMOS learning loop

Perceive → compress → expand → validate → express → store.

In HEARTLIGHT: observe signals → summarize the situation → generate multiple support hypotheses → check against the learner’s communication and context → offer choices → keep only useful, consented notes.

Stimming principle

Repetitive movement or sound may support regulation, attention, expression, or joy. The default response is not “stop the stim.” First ask whether it is safe, whether it is harming the learner or another person, and whether the environment can be changed.

About the engine

HEARTLIGHT adapts the COSMOS/CST local-first loop into an educational support tool. “Memory” here means bounded, reviewable records — not permanent child profiling. All recommendations are deterministic and inspectable in the source.

HEARTLIGHT is an educational support and accessibility tool, not a medical device, diagnostic system, psychotherapy replacement, crisis service, or substitute for qualified special-education/clinical judgment.
